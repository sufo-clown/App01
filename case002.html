CASE 002

Historical Investigation
THE ZODIAC
Northern California — 1968–1970

You are not entering a fictional crime scene.

This case really happened.

In the late 1960s, a series of murders and mysterious communications terrified communities around Northern California.

The killer was never conclusively identified.

He eventually became known by a name he gave himself: the Zodiac.

Your task is not to invent the answer. Your task is to follow the evidence.

You will move through the case in chronological order. At each stage, you will see what investigators knew, what they did not know, and what later evidence changed.

CHAPTER 1

December 20, 1968
THE FIRST CRIME
Lake Herman Road — California

It is late at night.

Seventeen-year-old David Faraday and sixteen-year-old Betty Lou Jensen are together near Lake Herman Road.

The area is quiet and relatively isolated.

Then something happens that investigators will spend years trying to understand.

Gunfire.

When investigators arrive, David Faraday and Betty Lou Jensen are dead.

There is no mysterious letter waiting for police. There is no killer calling himself Zodiac.

At this point, investigators are simply looking at a double homicide.

🔎 Your first question

If you were the investigator arriving that night, what would you need to establish first?

Correct.

There is a problem with the other answers: at this point, there is no known Zodiac identity, no known Zodiac symbol, and no letter. Investigators first have to understand the crime itself.

This distinction is important.

When people look backward at the Zodiac case today, they already know the entire story. Investigators in December 1968 did not.

They had to work forward from almost nothing.

And for months, there would be no obvious connection between this crime and what came later.

CHAPTER 2

July 4, 1969
BLUE ROCK SPRINGS
Vallejo, California

More than six months have passed since the Lake Herman Road murders.

Then, on the night of July 4, another attack occurs.

Darlene Ferrin and Michael Mageau are sitting in a car at Blue Rock Springs.

Another vehicle approaches.

The situation suddenly becomes violent.

Darlene Ferrin is killed. Michael Mageau survives the attack.

For investigators, the surviving victim becomes extremely important.

A living witness may be able to describe the attacker.

🧠 Evidence: The Witness

Michael Mageau survived and was able to provide investigators with information about the attacker.

But eyewitness evidence has limitations. A witness may remember important details, while other details may be uncertain.

Question

Why is a surviving witness valuable to an investigation?

Correct.

A witness can provide important information, but investigators still need to compare that information with physical evidence and other witnesses.

Then something happens that changes the investigation.

The police receive a telephone call.

The caller claims responsibility for both the Blue Rock Springs attack and the earlier Lake Herman Road murders.

Now investigators have a possible connection between the two crimes.

But they still don't know who the caller is.

And they have no idea that the case is about to become something much larger.

CHAPTER 3

July 31, 1969
SOMEONE WRITES
Bay Area Newspapers

Now the investigation enters unfamiliar territory.

Three newspapers receive letters.

The writer claims responsibility for the murders and demands that the newspapers publish his messages.

The letters are accompanied by portions of a strange cipher.

The writer threatens further violence if his demands are ignored.

For investigators, this creates a new problem.

The killer is not simply committing crimes.

Someone is deliberately communicating with the public.

The writer eventually begins using a name: “Zodiac.”

✉️ Evidence File A

Three newspapers receive different portions of the same 408-character cipher.

When the portions are combined, investigators have one complete encrypted message.

🧩 Your deduction

Why would an investigator compare the letters with evidence from earlier crimes?

Correct.

A claim of responsibility is evidence to examine, not automatic proof. Investigators need to compare details in the communications with known facts from the crimes.

The newspapers publish the cipher.

Now the public can see it too.

And somewhere in California, people begin trying to solve it.

The question becomes:

What is hidden inside the message?

CHAPTER 4

The 408 Cipher
BREAKING THE CODE
August 1969

The cipher looks almost impossible to read.

There are strange symbols arranged in rows.

There are no normal words.

No obvious sentence.

But two civilians, Donald and Bettye Harden, work on the problem.

They eventually find a solution.

The message is not a secret confession revealing a name. Instead, it contains a disturbing message from the writer.

🔐 What did the first cipher teach investigators?

It demonstrated that the writer was capable of creating a complicated-looking coded message.

But it did not provide investigators with a confirmed identity.

Important:
The 408 cipher was solved in 1969 by Donald and Bettye Harden. The final portion of the original cipher did not decode cleanly.

For the public, the cipher is fascinating.

For investigators, however, another question matters more:

Who is the person writing these messages?

Before investigators can answer that, another attack occurs.

CHAPTER 5

September 27, 1969
LAKE BERRYESSA
Napa County, California

Bryan Hartnell and Cecelia Shepard are at Lake Berryessa.

A man approaches them.

What follows is very different from the earlier shootings.

The attacker is wearing a distinctive hood.

Hartnell and Shepard are attacked with a knife.

Hartnell survives. Cecelia Shepard later dies from her injuries.

Before leaving, the attacker writes a message on the side of Hartnell's car.

The message includes dates connected with earlier attacks and the symbol later associated with the Zodiac.

🔎 New Evidence

Investigators now have something unusual: a crime-scene communication directly connected to the attacker.

This becomes another piece of the growing puzzle.

The case is becoming stranger.

Different locations.

Different victims.

Different methods.

But the communications suggest that the same person may be claiming responsibility.

And then, only a few weeks later, the case changes again.

CHAPTER 6

October 11, 1969
THE TAXI DRIVER
Presidio Heights — San Francisco

Paul Stine is working as a taxi driver.

He picks up a passenger.

The passenger asks to be taken to the Presidio Heights area.

At some point during the trip, the situation becomes deadly.

Paul Stine is killed.

The killer leaves the taxi.

Police begin searching the area.

🚨 A Critical Moment

Witnesses report seeing a man near the taxi.

Police receive a description.

But a communication error contributes to officers initially looking for the wrong description.

The investigation becomes one of the most important episodes in the entire case.

Three days later, the San Francisco Chronicle receives a letter.

Inside is a piece of Paul Stine's bloody shirt.

The writer claims responsibility for the murder.

Now investigators have a communication directly associated with the crime.

The killer is no longer simply a name in the newspapers. He is deliberately challenging investigators.

CHAPTER 7

October–November 1969
THE ZODIAC SPEAKS

The letters become an important part of the investigation.

The writer uses the name Zodiac.

He sends messages to newspapers.

He talks about previous crimes.

He threatens future violence.

And he continues to challenge police and the public.

At this point, investigators have a huge amount of information.

But information is not the same thing as an identity.

A person can claim to be responsible for crimes. A person can provide details. A person can write letters.

The challenge is proving who is actually behind them.

🧠 Think like an investigator

Which is stronger evidence?

Correct.

A claim can be important, especially if it contains information not publicly known, but investigators still need corroboration.

CHAPTER 8

November 8, 1969
THE 340 CIPHER

The San Francisco Chronicle receives another strange communication.

This time, the cipher contains 340 characters.

For decades, people will try to understand it.

Professional cryptographers, amateur investigators, and computer-assisted researchers all attempt to solve it.

But the message remains unread.

🔐 Evidence File

The cipher becomes known as the Z340.

Unlike the first cipher, it refuses to give up its meaning.

Years become decades.

1969 becomes 1970.

Then 1980.

1990.

2000.

Still unsolved.

2020

More than fifty years after the cipher was sent, David Oranchak, Sam Blake and Jarl Van Eycke solve the Z340.

The solution is confirmed as a genuine solution, but there is a disappointment.

The message does not reveal the killer's name.

Instead, it contains another strange message from the Zodiac.

The code was solved. The identity was not.

🔎 What does this mean?

Solving a cipher is not the same as solving a murder case.

The Z340 gives investigators and researchers another piece of the historical record, but it does not provide a confirmed identity for the Zodiac.

CHAPTER 9

The Search for a Name
WHO WAS THE ZODIAC?

Now we reach one of the most difficult parts of the case.

Investigators had to move from:

Crime scenes
Witness descriptions
Letters
Ciphers
Fingerprints and other physical evidence
Possible suspects

and try to determine whether all of it pointed to the same person.

👤 Persons of Interest

Over the decades, numerous people have been proposed as possible suspects.

One of the best-known was Arthur Leigh Allen.

However, being a person of interest is not the same thing as being proven to be the Zodiac.

Allen was never conclusively established as the killer.

⚠️ Investigator's Rule

Never turn:

"This person was investigated"

into:

"This person was proven guilty."

This is where many Zodiac theories become complicated.

Researchers have proposed different suspects, different interpretations of letters, different connections between crimes, and different readings of the ciphers.

Some theories are interesting.

But an interesting theory is not automatically established fact.

Case Report
THE TRUTH

The Zodiac case is unusual because part of the mystery has actually been solved.

Investigators established a series of murders and attacks. The killer communicated with newspapers. Some of those communications were authenticated. The 408 cipher was solved shortly after it was sent. The 340 cipher was finally solved in 2020.

But one of the biggest questions remains.

Who was the person behind the Zodiac name?

There have been suspects and theories. There have been proposed solutions to other cryptograms. There have been decades of investigation.

But there is no universally accepted identification that establishes the Zodiac's identity beyond reasonable dispute.

That means the correct conclusion is not:

"I know who the Zodiac was."

The historically responsible conclusion is:

The identity remains unconfirmed.
